Seeking a part-time PhD candidate intern to work with researchers in the NLR Center for Integrated Mobility Sciences on simulation, optimization, and data-driven modeling of freight terminals, rail networks, and other logistics-intensive sites such as mines. Work will center on ALTRIOS, NLR’s open-source rail system simulation tool, and ALTRIOS-LIFTS, an event-based framework for simulating operations, equipment, and energy use at multi-modal freight terminals and other work sites. Specific tasks may include: . Assist NLR researchers on projects providing insights into freight and logistics systems, with a focus on how site operations, equipment choices, and infrastructure investments affect throughput, energy use, and cost. . Develop and enhance simulation models of multi-modal freight terminals (rail, truck, and marine) and other logistics sites such as mines, representing process steps, equipment fleets, shift schedules, and charging or refueling infrastructure. . Model interactions between freight terminals and the broader rail system, including train arrivals and departures, terminal and yard capacity, dwell times, and how terminal operations affect line-haul performance and energy use. . Formulate and implement mathematical optimization and scheduling approaches for problems such as equipment assignment, train and crane scheduling, fleet sizing, and charging infrastructure sizing. . Apply machine learning and AI methods to tasks such as calibrating simulation models against real-world data, building surrogate models for rapid scenario analysis, and identifying patterns in operational data. . Process and analyze real-world operational datasets, such as equipment data logging, terminal activity records, and train movement data, and develop data visualizations and interactive dashboards that communicate results to technical and industry stakeholders. . Document methods and assumptions, contribute to open-source code, and assist with preparing peer-reviewed publications along with high-quality technical reports and presentations. . . .
