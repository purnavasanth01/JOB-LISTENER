## Job description (verbatim)

General Matter enriches uranium and develops low-cost enrichment services in the United States. The Embedded Software Engineering Intern will help develop safety-critical embedded software, firmware, control architectures, and data acquisition systems that operate enrichment equipment, while collaborating with multidisciplinary engineering teams on integration, debugging, testing, and commissioning.. . Responsibilities. Assist in the development of safety-critical embedded software that powers General Matter's industrial systems with reliability and precision. Help build low-level firmware in C/C++, Rust, and/or PLCs to control electromechanical actuators, process high-speed sensor data, and execute fault-tolerant control logic. Support the design of control architectures, state machines, and data acquisition systems for mission-critical hardware. Contribute to automated build, test, and validation infrastructure for embedded software. Partner closely with hardware, electrical, and controls engineers to bring up new systems, integrate firmware with hardware, and troubleshoot electromechanical challenges. Assist with debugging real-world embedded systems using oscilloscopes, logic analyzers, protocol analyzers, and other laboratory instrumentation. Support integrated system testing and commissioning of embedded software running on production equipment. . Skills. Current undergraduate or graduate student pursuing a degree in Computer Science, Computer Engineering, Electrical Engineering, or a similar discipline. Development experience in C, C++, Golang, Python, or Rust. General understanding of embedded or low-level systems development. Collaborative team member who thrives in fast-paced, multidisciplinary environments and is eager to learn. Strong understanding of engineering fundamentals. Ability to work extended hours and weekends as necessary. Exposure to data acquisition system setup, maintenance, or development. Familiarity with electrical hardware troubleshooting or PCBA bring-up. Coursework or project experience in digital signal processing. Exposure to low-level communication protocol debugging.
