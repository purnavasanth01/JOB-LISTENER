## Job description (verbatim)

Who we are . Neros is a defense technology company rebuilding America’s drone industrial base. We design and manufacture high-performance unmanned systems that are tested in combat, iterated at startup speed, and built at massive scale. Our team culture is fast, hands-on, and obsessed with closing the gap between design and deployment.. As drones transform the character of warfare, Neros is delivering the systems the West needs to compete on the modern battlefield and deter the adversaries of democracy. We’re hiring engineers, operators, and builders who want to move fast, take on extreme ownership, and get capability into the hands of warfighters in months, not years.. What you will be doing . Neros builds first-person-view drones for the modern battlefield. In just 2 years, Neros has built the highest rate drone production line in America, building over 1000 Archer UAVs per month.. . Firmware is what makes those aircraft fly. Neros firmware engineers write the embedded software that runs on every processor in the system — the flight controller that keeps the aircraft stable, the radios that carry command and video, the ground station the operator holds, and the payloads the aircraft carries. Our systems are built on small, low-cost bare-metal microcontrollers and embedded Linux, and they are designed for high volume and low cost. Firmware is a key part of every Neros electrical system, so firmware engineers work very closely with electrical engineers.. . As a firmware intern, you will own a real project on a real product — not a side task invented for an intern. You will write code that runs on hardware, debug problems that only appear on the bench, and see what you build fly. Neros firmware engineers own their work from start to finish, and interns are no exception: you'll help define what you're building, build it, test it, and hand it off working. Interns are placed on a team based on their strengths and what's in flight that term — flight controls, ground software, the embedded platform, or test and simulation.. Responsibilities . Write and debug embedded C/C++ on resource-constrained microcontrollers, or Python tooling that supports them. Bring up and test firmware on real hardware — flash it, instrument it, and find out why it doesn't work yet. Debug hardware-coupled problems using oscilloscopes, logic analyzers, and serial traces — failures that don't reproduce in a debugger. Write unit and integration tests for the firmware you build. Work with embedded communication interfaces — UART, SPI, I2C, CAN, Ethernet. Use git and participate in code review: open pull requests, respond to feedback, and review others' work. Collaborate with electrical, mechanical, test, and manufacturing engineers to get your work integrated into a product. Document what you built so the next person can pick it up. You should have the following . Working toward a Bachelor's or Master's degree in Computer Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Computer Science, or a related field — or equivalent personal projects. Programming experience in C or C++, and familiarity with Python. Exposure to embedded systems — microcontrollers, development boards, or hardware projects — through coursework, personal projects, clubs, or prior internships. Hands-on instinct: you have built something physical that runs code, and you have debugged it when it didn't work. Comfortable with ambiguity — you can take a loosely defined problem and make progress without being told each step. Familiarity with git. Nice to have . Personal projects involving drones, RC aircraft, robotics, or embedded hardware — bonus if you can show us. Flight stack exposure — Betaflight, PX4, ArduPilot, or similar. Experience with an RTOS (FreeRTOS, Zephyr, or similar) or embedded Linux. Soldering and rework skills; comfort with a bench and test equipment. Test automation or CI experience — pytest, hardware-in-the-loop setups, or build pipelines. Controls or signal-processing coursework — PID, filtering, sensor fusion. Competition team experience (SAE, Formula SAE, RoboMaster, drone racing, CubeSat, rocketry). Open-source contributions to embedded or drone projects. You fly — FPV, racing, or fixed-wing RC.
