## Job description (verbatim)

Teradata is hiring a UX Design Intern (AI-Focused) to take a design problem from initial framing through a working prototype. The intern will research user needs, design and test AI-focused experiences, build interactive prototypes, and collaborate with cross-functional teams to prepare and present design work.. . Responsibilities. Understand the problem. Partner with product managers, engineers, researchers, and data partners to understand user needs, business goals, and technical constraints, then turn an ambiguous need into a clear problem statement and a plan to test your ideas. Research and iterate. Join interviews and usability tests, create sketches, wireframes, and interactive prototypes in Figma, and validate multiple solutions through feedback. Design AI-focused experiences. Create flows and interfaces for conversational assistants, agents, and AI-assisted workflows, including prompts, responses, error states, and handoffs between people and AI. Design for trust. Make AI behavior understandable through transparency, explanations, and human-in-the-loop controls, so enterprise users know what AI did and why. Build, don't just draw. Use AI tools and front-end code to turn designs into interactive prototypes that engineers and customers can try, often within days. Deliver ready-to-build work. Follow Teradata's design system, prepare assets and documentation for engineering handoff, and document your design decisions. Collaborate and present. Join design critiques and cross-functional reviews, incorporate feedback, and demo your work to UX and product leaders at the end of the program. . Skills. You must be a UC San Diego Department of Cognitive Science student graduating in December 2027 or May/June 2028, with a portfolio that shows how you think and build. Currently enrolled in the UC San Diego Department of Cognitive Science, in any specialization, with an expected graduation of December 2027 or May/June 2028. A design portfolio. At least two projects that show your full design thinking process: problem framing, research, ideation, iteration, and outcome. Course projects, capstones, hackathons, research projects, and prior internships all count. Proven design thinking. You can explain why you made each decision, what you tested, what failed, and what you changed. Design fundamentals. A working understanding of user flows, layout, typography, and interaction patterns. Hands-on AI experience. You have designed or built something with AI, such as a conversational interface, an LLM-powered prototype, prompt design, or work with AI-assisted coding tools. Prototyping skills. Proficiency in Figma or a similar design tool, plus working knowledge of front-end code (HTML, CSS, and JavaScript or React) to build interactive prototypes. Research fundamentals. Experience planning and running interviews or usability studies and turning findings into design decisions. Craft and judgment. You value attention to detail while understanding tradeoffs and constraints, and you want to learn how products are built end to end. Curiosity and ownership. You are curious about users and how design solves real problems, comfortable working through ambiguity and asking questions, quick to iterate on feedback, and you take ownership of your work. Clear communication. You explain your design decisions in written, visual, and verbal form, and you communicate openly with engineers and non-designers. Experience designing for data, analytics, or enterprise software, where users handle complex tasks and high stakes. Experience working within or contributing to a design system. Familiarity with agent frameworks, Model Context Protocol (MCP), or retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and how they shape the user experience. Basic Python or SQL, enough to explore data or call an AI model from a prototype. Knowledge of accessibility standards, such as WCAG, and how to apply them. Experience with data visualization or conversational design. Coursework or research in human-computer interaction, cognitive modeling, or human-AI interaction. Mental models. What does a user believe an AI agent can and cannot do, and how do we shape that belief honestly?. Attention and memory. How much can someone take in from an AI response before they stop reading it?. Decision-making and trust. When do people rely on AI, and when should they double-check it?. Human-AI interaction. How do people and AI share work, and where should a person stay in control?. .
